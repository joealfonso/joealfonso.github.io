Most AI evaluation decisions are design decisions

Someone decides what “good” means, who checks it, how many times, and against what. If a designer isn’t part of those calls, the experience inherits whatever someone else picked.

Published
Nov 22, 2026
Reading time
7 min read
Topic
AI evaluation
Best for
Model designers, AI product designers, design leaders

The short version

An eval is a behavior spec. The rubric and the people grading against it shape what ships as much as any screen does, so designers should own them.

  • Read before you define: what “good” means shows up in real outputs, not in a rubric written up front.
  • Score the pair: people and models together often do worse than either alone, and the interface decides which way it goes.
  • Distrust the headline number: one good run and one overall average both hide the people a model fails.
  • What to do: put the all-runs-pass rate and the worst group next to every headline score.

    The rubric is a spec

    Every eval is a ProxyA number you can measure that stands in for something you can’t, like a test score standing in for skill.. A BenchmarkA fixed set of test questions used to compare models on the same task. Popular ones get reused for years, so models can end up tuned to the test. stands in for useful software, and a helpfulness score stands in for whether someone found what they came for. Once people, or the model itself, start pushing on that number, it goes up and the thing it was supposed to measure doesn’t. Scaling Laws for Reward Model OveroptimizationOpenAI researchers optimized models against an automated grader and tracked what happened to real quality as the optimization pushed further.Gao, Schulman & Hilton · ICML 2023 showed this by tuning models to please an Reward modelA second AI model trained on human ratings so it can score outputs automatically. It stands in for people during training, which makes it a proxy too. while keeping a Gold scoreIn this study, a larger reward model played the part of real human judgment. The models were never optimized against it, so it showed whether quality improved for real. on the side. The grader’s score kept climbing. The hidden one rose for a while and then fell.

    That’s why I treat a rubric like any other spec. Writing “concise” or “admits uncertainty” into it shapes what ships as much as a component spec shapes a screen, so it needs definitions with examples and somebody who owns it. When a score jumps, my first question is what the cheapest way to cause that jump would be. If the answer is something users would hate, like padding answers for a grader that likes length, the rubric needs work.

    Read outputs before you define good

    Most teams write the criteria first and read outputs later. It usually goes the other way. Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human PreferencesA team led by UC Berkeley researchers watched nine practitioners use a tool for building AI graders and saw their criteria change as they graded.Shankar et al. · UIST 2024, grading changed what they thought “good” meant. Tone, or whether an answer admits what it doesn’t know, only showed up as a criterion after they’d read a pile of examples. The researchers called it Criteria driftYour definition of a good answer changes as you read more answers. That’s normal, but scores from before and after the change can’t be compared., and anyone who’s done synthesis will recognize it: you don’t write the headings on the affinity map before you read the sticky notes.

    Read a few dozen real outputs before you write anything down. After that, date every rubric change and re-grade the same set of examples each time, so a new rubric never passes for a better model.

    The graders need checking too. Pervasive Label Errors in Test Sets Destabilize Machine Learning BenchmarksResearchers from MIT and AWS checked the answer keys of ten widely used test sets, including ImageNet, and found mistakes in all of them.Northcutt, Athalye & Mueller · NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks of popular test sets have Label errorA wrong answer in the answer key. When the key is wrong, a model can lose points for being right. in them, sometimes enough to reshuffle which model comes out on top. Have two people grade the same sample on their own and see how often they agree. Keep the cases where they split. Those are usually the behaviors nobody has designed yet.

    Test the person and the model together

    Most AI products keep a person in the loop, so the result comes from the pair, and the interface decides whether someone catches a wrong answer or waves it through.

    This is the part I think designers should care about most.

    The research isn’t encouraging. When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysisMIT researchers combined the results of 106 experiments, published from 2020 to mid-2023, that compared people, AI, and the two working together.Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone · Nature Human Behaviour, 2024 found that people and AI working together usually did worse on decisions than the better of the two alone, though pairs did better on creative work. Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team PerformanceResearchers from the University of Washington and Microsoft Research tested whether AI explanations help a person and an AI decide better together.Bansal et al. · CHI 2021 didn’t fix it, because people trusted the AI more whether it was right or not. What helped were Cognitive forcing functionAn interaction that makes a person think before leaning on the AI, such as asking for their own answer first or making them click to reveal the suggestion. that ask people to make their own call before they see the AI’s. To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-makingA Harvard-led team had 199 people make decisions with an AI helper, comparing plain explanations against designs that slowed people down.Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos · CSCW 2021, those cut OverrelianceAccepting the AI’s answer when it’s wrong, usually because it’s often right or sounds sure of itself., even though people liked them less.

    People also can’t see where the The jagged frontierThe study’s name for the uneven edge of what AI does well. Some hard-looking tasks sit inside it and some easy-looking ones sit outside, with no visible line between them. is. Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and QualityResearchers from Harvard Business School and other schools worked with Boston Consulting Group on a field experiment. 758 BCG consultants did realistic work with or without GPT-4.Dell’Acqua et al. · HBS Working Paper 24-013, 2023, people using GPT-4OpenAI’s most capable model when the study ran in spring 2023. finished more tasks and did better work on the tasks the model was good at. On one task built to trip it up, 84% of people working without AI answered correctly. With AI, that dropped to somewhere between 60% and 70%. Test your actual tasks one by one, including the ones that seem too easy to bother with, and do it again whenever the model changes.

    Distrust the headline number

    One good answer isn’t a behavior

    A lot of evals run each test once, or keep the best of a few tries. That tells you what a model can do, not what someone gets on Tuesday when they ask the same thing they asked on Monday. There are two ways to score repeat tries. pass@kCounts a task as solved if at least one of k tries works. It comes from coding benchmarks, where each try can be checked against unit tests and the passing one kept. counts a task as solved if any try works, and pass^kCounts a task as solved only if all k tries work. It measures consistency, which is closer to what a person using the product experiences. only counts it if every try works. In τ-bench (tau-bench)A test where an AI agent helps simulated customers with retail and airline requests, using company tools and following company policy.Yao et al. · ICLR 2025, GPT-4oAn OpenAI model released in 2024 and one of the strongest available when τ-bench was published. solved about 61% of retail tasks on the first try. Asked to do the same task eight times, it nailed all eight less than a quarter of the time.

    When I review an eval plan, I ask for every case to run at least three times, with a few rewordings of each request, and both scores reported. How high the bar goes depends on what a failure costs. A brainstorming tool can live with some variance. A tool answering benefits questions can’t.

    The average is hiding somebody

    Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender ClassificationAn audit of face analysis products from IBM, Microsoft, and Face++ by Joy Buolamwini of the MIT Media Lab and Timnit Gebru of Microsoft Research.Buolamwini & Gebru · FAT* 2018 tested three commercial Gender classifierSoftware that looks at a photo of a face and labels it male or female. that looked fine overall, between 88% and 94% accurate. Broken down by group, they were wrong about lighter-skinned men less than 1% of the time and about darker-skinned women up to 35% of the time.

    To me this is accessibility work in a different form. We don’t ship an interface because the average user finished the task. We test with screen readers, because that’s where things break. Pick the groups that matter before you look at the overall number, and put the worst one right next to the average.

    Where the model runs matters too. A Human-Centered Evaluation of a Deep Learning System Deployed in Clinics for the Detection of Diabetic RetinopathyGoogle Health researchers watched nurses use an AI screening tool for diabetic eye disease in 11 clinics in Thailand.Beede et al. · CHI 2020, lighting and busy workflows hurt how it performed, and it rejected photos the nurses could realistically take. The model had passed its tests. The system hadn’t. Designers already own the methods that catch this, like contextual inquiry and small pilots.

    Where this argument breaks down

    Some of evaluation is research work, not design work. Error barsThe range a score could land in by chance. If two models’ ranges overlap, the gap between them may not be real. and Statistical significanceA check on whether a difference is bigger than random noise would produce. belong with people trained for them, and so does spotting Test contaminationTest questions that leak into a model’s training data, so a high score can reflect memory instead of skill.. A designer who waves those away does more harm than one who stays out of the room. Some of what I’ve cited is also thin. The criteria drift study watched nine people on one task, and it didn’t test the fixes I’m suggesting. Designers shouldn’t run the eval. They should own the parts that decide behavior, starting with the rubric and the people grading against it.

    Lessons learned

    1. Treat the rubric as a design artifact. Give it an owner and a version history with reasons.
    2. Read outputs before you write criteria. Keep a fixed reference set so a rubric change never passes for a model improvement.
    3. Score the pair, not the model. Look hardest at the cases where the model is wrong. That’s where the interface helps or hurts.
    4. Put the worst number next to the headline. Report the all-runs-pass rate beside the average and the worst group beside the overall score.
    5. Label your confidence. Mark each finding in an eval readout as well sourced, developing, or thin, so whoever makes the call knows how hard to lean on it.
