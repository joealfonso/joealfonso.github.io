Most AI evaluation decisions are design decisions

Someone decides what “good” means, who checks it, how many times, and against what. If a designer isn’t part of those calls, the experience inherits whatever someone else picked.

Published
Nov 22, 2026
Reading time
7 min read
Topic
AI evaluation
Best for
Model designers, AI product designers, design leaders

The short version

An eval is a behavior spec. The rubric, the raters, and the test conditions shape what ships as much as any screen does, so designers should own them.

  • Read before you define: what “good” means shows up in real outputs, not in a rubric written up front.
  • Score the pair: people and models together often do worse than either alone, and the interface decides which way it goes.
  • Distrust the headline number: one good run and one overall average both hide the people a model fails.
  • What to do: put the all-runs-pass rate and the worst group next to every headline score.

    The rubric is a spec

    Every eval is a ProxyA number you can measure that stands in for something you can’t, like a test score standing in for skill.. A BenchmarkA fixed set of test questions used to compare models on the same task. Popular ones get reused for years, so models can end up tuned to the test. stands in for useful software. A helpfulness score stands in for whether a person found what they came for. Once people, or the model itself, start pushing on the proxy, the number moves and the thing behind it doesn’t. Scaling Laws for Reward Model OveroptimizationOpenAI researchers optimized models against an automated grader and tracked what happened to real quality as the optimization pushed further.Gao, Schulman & Hilton · ICML 2023 optimized a model against a Reward modelA second AI model trained on human ratings so it can score outputs automatically. It stands in for people during training, which makes it a proxy too. while tracking a separate Gold scoreIn this study, a larger reward model played the part of real human judgment. The models were never optimized against it, so it showed whether quality improved for real.. The gold score went up, then turned and came back down while the proxy kept climbing.

    Writing “concise” or “acknowledges uncertainty” into a grading rubric shapes what ships just as much as a component spec shapes a screen. It deserves the same rigor: clear definitions, examples of pass and fail, and an owner. Before anyone celebrates a jump in a score, I ask what the cheapest way to raise that number would be. If the answer is something users would hate, like padded answers for a grader that rewards length, the rubric is wrong.

    A rubric is a behavior spec that happens to be written as a grading sheet.

    Read outputs before you define good

    Teams usually write criteria first and look at outputs second. Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human PreferencesA team led by UC Berkeley researchers watched nine practitioners use a tool for building AI graders and saw their criteria change as they graded.Shankar et al. · UIST 2024 watched practitioners grade model outputs and found that grading changed what they thought “good” meant. Criteria like tone, or whether an answer admits what it doesn’t know, showed up late. They called it Criteria driftYour definition of a good answer changes as you read more answers. That’s normal, but scores from before and after the change can’t be compared.. Designers know this from synthesis: you don’t write the headings on the affinity map before you’ve read the sticky notes. Read a few dozen real outputs first, version the rubric with a date and a reason for each change, and re-grade a fixed reference set whenever it moves.

    The people applying the rubric are a measurement too. Pervasive Label Errors in Test Sets Destabilize Machine Learning BenchmarksResearchers from MIT and AWS checked the answer keys of ten widely used test sets, including ImageNet, and found mistakes in all of them.Northcutt, Athalye & Mueller · NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks found Label errorA wrong answer in the answer key. When the key is wrong, a model can lose points for being right. in at least 3.3% of examples across ten widely used test sets, enough to change which models ranked higher. Have two people label the same sample on their own and measure how often they agree. Keep the disagreements. The cases where two careful raters split are usually the behaviors nobody has designed yet.

    Test the person and the model together

    Most AI products keep a person in the loop, so the outcome comes from the pair. This is the pattern I think designers should care about most, because the interface decides whether a person catches a wrong answer or waves it through.

    When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysisMIT researchers combined the results of 106 experiments, published from 2020 to mid-2023, that compared people, AI, and the two working together.Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone · Nature Human Behaviour, 2024 pooled 106 studies and found that, on average, human and AI combinations did worse than the better of the two working alone. The pairs lost ground on decision tasks and gained on content creation. Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team PerformanceResearchers from the University of Washington and Microsoft Research tested whether AI explanations help a person and an AI decide better together.Bansal et al. · CHI 2021 found that AI explanations made people more likely to accept the AI’s advice whether it was right or not. Explanations raised trust, not accuracy. To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-makingA Harvard-led team had 199 people make decisions with an AI helper, comparing plain explanations against designs that slowed people down.Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos · CSCW 2021 had better luck with Cognitive forcing functionAn interaction that makes a person think before leaning on the AI, such as asking for their own answer first or making them click to reveal the suggestion., small patterns that make a person commit to their own judgment before seeing the AI’s. They cut OverrelianceAccepting the AI’s answer when it’s wrong, usually because it’s often right or sounds sure of itself. compared with simple explanations.

    Trust also doesn’t know where the The jagged frontierThe study’s name for the uneven edge of what AI does well. Some hard-looking tasks sit inside it and some easy-looking ones sit outside, with no visible line between them. is. Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and QualityResearchers from Harvard Business School and other schools worked with Boston Consulting Group on a field experiment. 758 BCG consultants did realistic work with or without GPT-4.Dell’Acqua et al. · HBS Working Paper 24-013, 2023 ran field experiments with 758 consultants at Boston Consulting Group. On tasks the model handled well, people using GPT-4OpenAI’s most capable model when the study ran in spring 2023. finished 12.2% more tasks and worked 25.1% faster. On one task built so the model would get it wrong, 84.5% of the people without AI answered correctly, against roughly 60% to 70% of the people with it. Map that edge task by task, including tasks that seem too easy to test, and redo the map every time the model changes.

    Distrust the headline number

    One good answer isn’t a behavior

    A lot of evals run each case once, or report the best of several tries. That shows what a model can do, not what a person gets on Tuesday when they ask what they asked on Monday. There are two ways to score repeat tries. pass@kCounts a task as solved if at least one of k tries works. It comes from coding benchmarks, where each try can be checked against unit tests and the passing one kept. counts a task as solved if any one of k tries works. pass^kCounts a task as solved only if all k tries work. It measures consistency, which is closer to what a person using the product experiences. counts it only if every try works. In τ-bench (tau-bench)A test where an AI agent helps simulated customers with retail and airline requests, using company tools and following company policy.Yao et al. · ICLR 2025, a test where AI agents help simulated shoppers, GPT-4oAn OpenAI model released in 2024 and one of the strongest available when τ-bench was published. solved about 61% of retail tasks on a single try. Asked to solve the same task eight times, it managed all eight less than a quarter of the time.

    Inconsistency is an experience problem before it’s a metrics problem. When I review an eval plan, I ask for each case to run at least three times, with paraphrased versions of each task, and both rates reported. Where the bar sits depends on the cost of a failure. A brainstorming tool can live with variance. A tool that answers benefits questions can’t.

    The average is hiding somebody

    In Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender ClassificationAn audit of face analysis products from IBM, Microsoft, and Face++ by Joy Buolamwini of the MIT Media Lab and Timnit Gebru of Microsoft Research.Buolamwini & Gebru · FAT* 2018, Buolamwini and Gebru (2018) tested three commercial Gender classifierSoftware that looks at a photo of a face and labels it male or female. with overall accuracy between 87.9% and 93.7%. Error rates reached 34.7% for darker-skinned women and stayed at or under 0.8% for lighter-skinned men. To me this is accessibility work in another form. We don’t sign off on an interface because the average user finished the task. We test with screen readers and slow connections because that’s where things break. Define the groups that matter before you look at the overall number, and report the worst one next to the average.

    The same goes for where a model runs. A Human-Centered Evaluation of a Deep Learning System Deployed in Clinics for the Detection of Diabetic RetinopathyGoogle Health researchers watched nurses use an AI screening tool for diabetic eye disease in 11 clinics in Thailand.Beede et al. · CHI 2020 followed a diabetic eye screening system into 11 clinics in Thailand. Lighting and workflow affected how it performed, and its image quality rules rejected photos the clinics could realistically take. The model had been validated. The system hadn’t. Contextual inquiry, diary studies, and small pilots close that gap, and designers already own those methods.

    Where this argument breaks down

    Evaluation is also a research discipline, and some of it isn’t design work. Error barsThe range a score could land in by chance. If two models’ ranges overlap, the gap between them may not be real., Test contaminationTest questions that leak into a model’s training data, so a high score can reflect memory instead of skill., and Statistical significanceA check on whether a difference is bigger than random noise would produce. belong with people trained for them, and a designer who waves those away does more damage than one who stays out of the room. The evidence is uneven too. Criteria drift rests on thin evidence: the study covered nine practitioners on one task and didn’t test the fixes I suggest. I’m not arguing designers should run the eval. I’m arguing they should own the parts that decide behavior: the rubric, the raters, the pair, the groups, and the setting.

    Lessons learned

    1. Treat the rubric as a design artifact. Give it an owner, examples of pass and fail, and a version history with reasons.
    2. Read outputs before you write criteria. Keep a fixed reference set so a rubric change never passes for a model improvement.
    3. Score the pair, not the model. Look hardest at the cases where the model is wrong. That’s where the interface helps or hurts.
    4. Put the worst number next to the headline. Report the all-runs-pass rate beside the average and the worst group beside the overall score.
    5. Label your confidence. Mark each finding in an eval readout as well sourced, developing, or thin, so whoever makes the call knows how hard to lean on it.
